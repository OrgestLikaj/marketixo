7 reasons your website isn’t bringing you clients (and how to fix them)

Visitors arrive but nobody calls? These are the seven most common reasons business websites fail to generate enquiries — and the fix for each one.

5 October 2026 · 2 min read

A website that looks good but doesn’t bring enquiries is an expensive business card. In almost every audit we do, the problem comes down to the same handful of issues. Here they are — and what to do about each one.

1. Nobody can tell what you do in five seconds

Visitors decide very quickly whether they are in the right place. If your homepage opens with “Welcome to our website” or a vague slogan, they leave.

Fix: Your main headline should say what you do, for whom, and where. “Wedding photography in Munich” beats “Capturing moments that matter” every time.

2. There is no clear next step

If the only way to contact you is a small “Contact” link in the menu, many people won’t bother.

Fix: Give every page one obvious action — a quote form, a call booking or a WhatsApp button — and repeat it at the end of each page.

3. It is slow, especially on mobile

Most of your visitors are on their phone, often on a mobile connection. Every extra second of loading time costs you visitors, and Google takes speed into account in its rankings.

Fix: Compress and resize images, remove plugins you don’t need, and use good hosting. Test with PageSpeed Insights.

4. Google can’t find it

A beautiful website with no visitors does nothing. Common causes: one page trying to rank for everything, missing page titles and descriptions, or pages that are accidentally blocked from indexing.

Fix: Create one page per service, write a unique title and meta description for each, and set up Google Search Console to see what Google sees.

5. There is no proof

People don’t trust claims, they trust evidence. “High quality service” means nothing; “Helped 40 local businesses double their enquiries” means something.

Fix: Add real project examples, client logos, short testimonials and — whenever you have them — numbers.

6. It talks about you instead of the client

“We were founded in…”, “Our team…”, “Our values…”. Visitors care about one thing: whether you can solve their problem.

Fix: Rewrite your key pages around the client’s problem and the result you deliver. Mention yourself as the guide, not the hero.

7. Nobody is measuring anything

If you don’t know how many people visit, where they come from and how many contact you, you can’t improve any of it.

Fix: Set up privacy-friendly analytics and track form submissions, calls and WhatsApp clicks as conversions.
